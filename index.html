Overview

I built a cloth simulator that models fabric as a grid of point masses connected by springs. I used damped Verlet integration to advance the simulation, added collisions with spheres and planes, and used spatial hashing to handle collisions between folds, compared how stiffness, density, and damping affect the cloth as it falls and settles, and rendered the cloth with diffuse and Blinn-Phong lighting, plaid texture mapping, bump and displacement mapping, and environment reflections. For extra credit, I made an iredescent shader that shifts from teal to violet and to pink as the viewing angle changes!

Part 1: Masses and springs

I built a grid of evenly spaced masses in row-major order and pinned the points listed in the scene. Structural springs connect adjacent masses along rows and columns. Shearing springs connect diagonal neighbors. Bending springs span two grid steps along rows and columns.

Part 2: Simulation via numerical integration

Each step resets the forces to gravity and other external forces, then adds Hooke's law forces from enabled springs. Bending springs use 20% of the stiffness of the other springs. Damped Verlet integration updates the unpinned masses. A final pass shortens springs stretched beyond 110% of their rest length and leaves pinned endpoints fixed.

I varied one setting at a time in scene/pinned2.json. The defaults were 5000 N/m, 15 g/cm^2 as displayed in the simulator, and 0.2% damping. Each run used 90 frames per simulated second and 30 steps per frame.

Spring constant

At 500 N/m, the cloth developed more ripples during the fall and hung lower once it settled. At 25,000 N/m, it stayed smoother during the fall and sagged less between the pins at rest.

Density

The cloth started falling at the same gravitational acceleration in all three runs. The 3 g/cm^2 cloth deformed less as the springs took up the load and had the shallowest sag at rest. The 40 g/cm^2 cloth stretched more and hung lower.

Damping

At 0.05% damping, the free edge swung past vertical and kept oscillating after the default run had nearly settled. At 1.0%, the cloth descended slowly with little overshoot. All three runs approached the same hanging shape.

Part 3: Handling collisions with other objects

After updating positions with Verlet integration, I check each unpinned mass against every collision object. For a sphere, I compare the distance from the center with the radius. A mass at or inside the surface gets a contact point by projecting its position radially onto the sphere.

For a plane, I use signed distances from prev and current positions to detect crossings. I intersect the segment between those positions with the plane and offset the contact point toward the side the mass came from. For both shapes, I move the mass from its previous position toward the contact target by a fraction of 1 - friction.

Sphere

In scene/sphere.json, the 500 N/m cloth wraps closely around the sphere and its corners hang lowest. The default 5000 N/m produces an intermediate drape. At 50,000 N/m, the cloth stretches less and its edges flare farther out. All three captures use Normal shading and show the settled drape after about ~10 s.

Part 4: Handling self-collisions

After Verlet integration, I rebuild a spatial hash from the updated positions. The cell width and height are three times the corresponding cloth dimension divided by the number of masses along that axis. The cell depth is the max of the two. I round scaled coordinates down and pack the three cell indices into a key.

Each unpinned mass checks the other masses in its bucket. For every neighbor closer than twice the cloth thickness, I compute a displacement that would restore that separation. I average those displacements and divide by the number of simulation steps per frame before moving the mass.

Part 5: Shaders

A shader program is GPU code that runs at stages of the rendering pipeline. The vertex shader positions each vertex and passes along values such as its world position, normal, and UV coords. The rasterizer interpolates those values across each triangle. The fragment shader uses the interpolated values and shared inputs, such as lights and textures, to compute the fragment colors.

Blinn-Phong lighting

Ambient light adds a constant base color, diffuse light depends on how directly the surface faces the light and falls off with the square of the distance, specular light creates a highlight where the surface normal aligns with the halfway vector between the light and the viewer, and the full shader adds the three contributions. Each view here uses the same settled cloth from scene/sphere.json.

Texture mapping

I got a plaid texture with an alternating weave pattern. The fragment shader samples it at the cloth's interpolated UV coordinates, so the pattern follows the folds!

Bump and displacement mapping

Both shaders use the red channel of the brick height map textures/texture_3.png. The fragment shader samples one texel over in each UV direction and uses the height differences to tilt the normal before lighting and bump mapping leaves the mesh silhouette intact. Displacement also moves each vertex along its normal, so the height map changes the outline.

At 16x16, the sphere is visibly faceted and displacement can only move a few vertices across each brick, and at 128 x 128, the displaced surface follows the brick relief more closely. The fine details from bump mapping are visible at both resolutions likely because the height differences are sampled per fragment. The four views use Normal 32 and Height 0.12 (as seen in sim)

Environment reflections

The mirror shader reflects the ray from the camera about the surface normal and samples the cubemap in the reflected direction. The sphere bends the reflection continuously, while the cloth folds stretch it into narrow bands.

Part 5 Extra Credit: Iredescent Shader

The custom shader produces a color-changing woven-fabric appearance by scaling UV coordinates to generate a repeating crosshatched thread pattern using cosine functions, with fwidth and smoothstep to reduce aliasing.

I calculates the view direction, face-forwarded surface normal, and orthogonal tangent and vectors to establish a local shading basis. The material color transitions between teal, purple, and pink according to the grazing angle (the angle between the surface normal and view direction), creating an angle-dependent iredescent effect. At the end, the woven pattern modulates the material color, which is multiplied by u_color.rgb and combined with the lighting to produce the final dense view!
